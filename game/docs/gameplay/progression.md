# Progression, identity and specialization

## Core progression philosophy

Rustwater should make progression feel like an expanding set of possibilities, not merely a larger number next to the same action.

Numerical progression supports the game. **Capability progression** should define its memorable milestones.

## XP and level

### Implemented

Characters gain XP and levels. Level is visible and persistent.

Current level does **not** directly increase fishing power/control or gate the Rusted Key thread. It primarily communicates investment/seniority.

Potential future uses include prestige, social recognition, event eligibility, giveaways or broad experience requirements. Do not turn level into a universal strength multiplier without a concrete design reason.

## Capability progression

Long-term progression should unlock things such as:

- new water/biomes/regions;
- hostile environments;
- new equipment/device categories;
- detection of hidden entities;
- transport/vehicles;
- underwater/vertical/dimensional traversal;
- decoding unknown information;
- manipulating environmental conditions;
- professions or specialized interactions;
- access earned through reputation/relationships;
- infrastructure or social/world capabilities.

A good unlock changes what the player can perceive, reach, understand or do.

## Specialization

Long term, no one character should master everything.

The exact structure is intentionally open. Candidate ingredients include:

- talents;
- traits;
- professions;
- equipment investment;
- reputation;
- knowledge/discoveries;
- wealth/economic specialization;
- player-built infrastructure;
- social relationships;
- mutually exclusive or opportunity-cost choices.

Rigid permanent classes are not required. Specialization should emerge from meaningful investment and choices rather than forcing a class taxonomy too early.

Multiple characters per account remain open; only add them when they improve roleplay/specialization more than they undermine meaningful opportunity cost.

## Discovery as progression

Discoveries should unlock more than collection percentages when appropriate:

- clues to locations;
- recipes/interactions;
- better observations;
- NPC dialogue/relationships;
- access to factions or activities;
- equipment knowledge;
- world-state changes;
- art/gallery entries;
- social recognition.

Implicit player knowledge is also progression even when no meter changes.

## Reputation and relationships

Reputation/relationships are long-term progression dimensions, not merely narrative decoration. Trust can become access, information, assistance, opportunities, scenes, specialized goods, world influence or collaboration.

Exact reputation levels/models remain open; see [`../narrative/characters-and-relationships.md`](../narrative/characters-and-relationships.md).

## Titles

### Implemented

Titles are non-power social identity. Current options are:

- No title;
- Angler;
- Naturalist;
- Relic Hunter;
- Harbor Hand;
- Covekeeper;
- Shoalbound.

Unlocks derive from real persistent accomplishments such as catches, discoveries, the Rusted Key/lighthouse thread, Harbor Board milestones and group-shoal participation. The equipped title appears on Home and currently grants no gameplay bonus.

This separation between **identity/status** and **power** is intentional.

## Harbor Board

### Implemented one-time milestones

- **First Haul** — catch five fish; 25 coins + 20 XP.
- **Field Notes** — discover six species; 5 Glow Larvae + 30 XP.
- **Deep Water** — land a difficulty-11+ fish at Broken Breakwater; 50 coins + 35 XP.

Milestone progress derives from authoritative history and rewards are transactionally claimable once.

### Implemented repeating contract

Each fictional game day selects a deterministic Old Harbor species. A matching stored specimen is required. Turn-in consumes the smallest matching specimen, preserving better catches, then grants coins + XP.

This proves a repeatable loop that links collection, economy and the world without needing a generic quest engine.

## Long-horizon goals

The same progression model should support:

- immediate goals (one catch/action);
- session goals (gear, a clue, a new record);
- exploration branches;
- relationship/faction arcs;
- collections and rare variants;
- seasonal/social goals;
- infrastructure/world projects;
- multi-year identity and discovery.

Short interactions and long goals should remain attached to the same persistent character/world.
