# Visual direction

## Brand image

Rustwater should look like a **cozy, lived-in maritime fantasy world with quiet mystery and genuine adventure**, not a generic fishing app.

Fishing is visually recognizable because it is the player's starting activity, but key art should also imply exploration, relationships, weather, relics, travel, social life, progression and a much larger world.

The currently favored rendering direction is polished **anime/anime-inspired game illustration**, especially for characters and cinematic wallpapers. That rendering choice can evolve; the deeper brand pillars matter more than one renderer.

## Visual pillars

- cozy maritime fantasy;
- weathered harbor life;
- exploration and discovery;
- social adventure;
- quiet mystery rather than default horror;
- dynamic weather/time-of-day mood;
- an inhabited world with history;
- polished modern indie-game presentation;
- room for the setting to grow into stranger and more fantastical places later.

## Rustwater environment language

Recurring visual vocabulary:

- weathered wooden piers;
- ropes, nets, floats and tackle;
- lanterns and warm window light;
- tackle stalls and notice boards;
- small boats and working harbor details;
- sea spray and wet wood;
- dark/coastal rock;
- gulls;
- fog and low cloud;
- reflective water;
- lighthouses and distant coastlines;
- relics/clues/strange objects used sparingly;
- signs that people live and work here.

Do not require all motifs in every image. Repetition should build identity, not clutter.

## Palette

Typical Rustwater base:

- ocean/navy blues;
- muted teal;
- misty gray;
- weathered wood/brown;
- cream/off-white;
- rust/copper/brass accents;
- warm lantern gold / mustard yellow as strong focal warmth.

Weather/time variants can substantially shift the image while preserving the underlying world identity: foggy blue dawn, rainy gray afternoon, golden harbor dusk, lantern night, moonlit cove, storm-lit coast.

Mara's yellow/blue/teal palette can act as a recognizable brand anchor without forcing every location/object into the same colors.

## Character art

Characters should feel specific to the world, with practical silhouettes and strong recognition anchors. Avoid generic costume tropes unless a particular culture/character intentionally uses them.

For Mara, [`../narrative/mara-reed.md`](../narrative/mara-reed.md) is canonical and takes precedence over broad style experimentation.

Future major NPCs should eventually receive similarly compact character bibles covering:

- fixed physical traits;
- silhouette/outfit anchors;
- palette;
- iconic objects;
- age/species;
- expression set;
- outfit variants;
- narrative role/personality;
- things generation must not drift.

## What to avoid by default

- generic mobile-game ad composition;
- casino/reward-pop-up energy;
- “anime girl + fish” with no worldbuilding;
- childish toy/cartoon proportions unless a deliberate style variant;
- meme aesthetics as core identity;
- excessive fantasy armor unrelated to the setting;
- modern urban fashion pasted into a harbor fantasy without context;
- grim survival realism as the default mood;
- UI clutter used to make a promo image look “game-like”;
- giant logos/emblems on believable in-world clothing;
- visual sexualization replacing character identity.

## Environment vs protagonist

For key art/wallpapers, the character is often the focal point but the **world should be the co-star**. Details in the environment should imply systems and stories beyond the exact action shown.

A fishing image can include a distant lighthouse, busy notice board, strange relic on the satchel, weather change, other harbor figures or inaccessible coastline. The viewer should feel there is somewhere else to go afterward.

## Master world prompt block

This can be reused as a stable brand layer for generative art:

> Create art for **Rustwater**, a Telegram-native social sandbox MMO RPG set initially in a persistent coastal world. Rustwater is a cozy yet deep maritime fantasy adventure centered on a weathered harbor and mysterious surrounding waters, but fishing is only the entry point into a much larger world. The finished-game vision includes exploration, travel, changing weather and time, memorable NPCs and relationships, reputation, tasks and contracts, crafting, equipment, records, titles, relics, rare discoveries, social group events, world history and eventually increasingly strange regions and capabilities. The world should feel inviting, atmospheric, lived-in, socially alive and quietly mysterious. Use maritime environmental storytelling—weathered piers, ropes, nets, lanterns, tackle stalls, boats, sea spray, coastal rocks, gulls, mist, reflective water, lighthouses, distant coastlines and subtle clues to hidden things. Communicate discovery, progression and the promise of a world larger than the foreground. Avoid generic mobile-game advertising, casino energy, prototype/bot aesthetics, childish clutter, or reducing the image to a simple fish-catching minigame.

Then append the specific character/scene, rendering style, mood, aspect ratio/cropping and asset-use requirements.

## Asset-type guidance

### Wallpaper / key art

Use environmental depth, one strong focal story, and useful negative space. The image should make the viewer want to inhabit/explore the world.

### Character portrait

Prioritize recognition anchors, expression and role. Background can imply location without competing with the face.

### Profile/avatar/icon

One central silhouette/emblem, strong contrast, minimal fine detail. Do not try to summarize every system in a tiny crop.

### Feature promo

Show the feature clearly but include enough broader-world context that it still feels like Rustwater rather than an isolated mechanic.

### Collectible/gallery art

Can be more intimate, surprising or character-specific because it is earned inside the game rather than serving as first-impression marketing.
