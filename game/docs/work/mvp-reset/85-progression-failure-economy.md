# Progression, failure and economy

These areas are intentionally not fully designed yet, but the reset discussion established several directional constraints that a fresh session must preserve.

## Locked: failure matters without casual catastrophic loss

Failure should create consequences and stories without casually deleting months of investment because of Telegram/network latency, unclear UX or one ordinary mistake.

The intended severity is closer to recoverable MMO/sandbox friction (the broad WoW/Terraria direction discussed) than default Minecraft-style catastrophic inventory loss.

Useful consequence classes include:

- lost opportunity;
- consumed resources;
- equipment wear/repair;
- temporary injury/debuff/state;
- expedition failure or recovery cost;
- positioning/travel consequences;
- explicit high-risk stakes when the player knowingly opts into them.

The exact death model remains open. Do not infer from this direction that death must be consequence-free.

## Locked: fishing is not the progression spine

Rods, bait and fishing outcomes are ordinary world items/interactions. Character progression must not assume fishing XP, fish collection tiers or fishing equipment are the universal advancement model.

Knowledge, capabilities, relationships, reputation, tools/equipment, discoveries, profession/skill development and social/world achievements may all contribute to progression. The exact composition remains open.

## Leaning: simple scalable internal currency

The game should first have a coherent internal RPG economy. A WoW/Terraria-like denomination presentation (for example higher/lower units formatted from one underlying integer balance) is a strong direction because it is compact and scalable.

Exact names/symbols are open. Gold/silver/platinum-style letters or icons were discussed as presentation possibilities, not locked lore.

Routine spendable currency is currently preferred as ledger/accounting state rather than backpack coin stacks; collectible or historical coins may still be normal item instances.

## Locked: mundane junk is allowed to be mundane

The world should contain actual junk and low-value ordinary finds. Not every boot, spoon or bottle should secretly start a quest. Mundane outcomes make exceptional ones credible.

Inventory/economy UX must make junk manageable at scale, including bulk disposal/selling and an aggregate value where appropriate. Exact junk classification and vendor-pricing rules remain open.

## Open progression questions

- Is there a universal character level at all, and if so what does it summarize?
- Which progression is skill/profession based versus capability/knowledge/equipment based?
- How are specialization choices expressed without prematurely locking the player into a class?
- What progression is account-wide versus character-specific?
- How much numerical stat detail is exposed by default?
- How do records, discoveries, reputation and relationships contribute to long-term goals?

## Open death/failure questions

- What exactly happens on ordinary character death/incapacitation?
- Where/how does recovery occur?
- What can be lost, damaged or temporarily inaccessible?
- Can party members rescue/recover another character?
- Which activities can advertise opt-in high-loss stakes?

## Open economy questions

- Exact denominations and player-facing currency notation.
- Vendor pricing and regional demand.
- Direct trade versus market/auction infrastructure in the MVP.
- Item sinks, repair/crafting costs and inflation controls only when the first real economy needs them.
- Whether any account-level entitlements/monetization ever touch the in-world economy; they are not required for the game to make sense.
