# Progression and Specialization

> **Status:** mixed settled/open

## XP and level

The current decision is simple:

- characters gain XP;
- level is visible;
- level currently does not directly make the character stronger.

Level communicates investment/seniority and may later matter for social recognition, events, giveaways, prestige, or broad eligibility.

Do not casually turn level into a generic damage/power multiplier merely because RPGs usually do.

## Capability progression

The memorable progression should unlock what the player **can do**:

- reach new waters;
- survive new environments;
- use different tackle/device categories;
- detect hidden entities;
- traverse dimensions;
- operate transport;
- decode information;
- manipulate conditions.

## Specialization

Long term, no character should master everything.

The exact mechanism is open, but likely ingredients include:

- talents;
- traits;
- professions;
- equipment investment;
- reputation;
- knowledge;
- infrastructure;
- wealth;
- social relationships;
- mutually exclusive/opportunity-cost choices.

Rigid classes are not required.

## Titles

The v4 prototype used titles as **non-power social identity**. This matches the desired separation between status and capability.

Examples included:

- Angler;
- Naturalist;
- Relic Hunter;
- Harbor Hand;
- Covekeeper;
- Shoalbound.

This is a useful pattern to preserve even if the exact title list changes.
